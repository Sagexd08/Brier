# Ablation report

Every enhancement in Phases A–G, measured against the existing baseline on
the same protocol, with negative and null results reported at the same
visibility as positive ones. Generated by `scripts/19_phaseH_ablation_report.py`
from the artifacts in `artifacts/ablation/`; do not edit by hand.

The baseline throughout is the shipped pipeline: overfit XGBoost base model,
one-parameter temperature-scaling calibration head, SHAP evidence vector.
The protocol is the pinned 10-seed list in `config.EVAL_SEEDS`, always run in
full. The XGBoost arm reproduces the proposal's headline figures exactly
(ECE 0.1853 → 0.0870), which is the check that the protocol really is the
same one rather than merely a similar one.

## Summary

| Phase | Enhancement | Tier | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Deep tabular NN + heterogeneous ensemble | 1 | **Null** — no significant calibration gain, costs accuracy |
| B | Split conformal prediction | **2** | **Adopted** — passes the circuit gate; coverage tracks target |
| C | Deep ensembles for epistemic uncertainty | 1 | **Negative** — the signal actively hurts calibration |
| D | Counterfactual explanations | 1 | **Adopted** — 100% found, actionable, deterministic |
| E | GNN collusion detection | **3, and thinner** | **Wired to enforcement while still unvalidated** |
| F | On-chain operator reputation | 3 (inherited) | **Adopted** — auditable aggregation over tier-3 inputs |
| G | Model-version registry | 1.5 (content integrity) | **Adopted** — closes a naming gap, not a trust gap |

Two of seven phases did not earn their place. Both are kept in the
repository as evidence and neither is wired into the default pipeline.

## Phase A — deep tabular base model and ensemble (Tier 1)

Entity-embedding MLP over 12 categorical and 7 numeric features, ensembled
with XGBoost in probability space so the calibration head still receives a
single scalar margin.

| Model | Uncalibrated ECE | Calibrated ECE | Calibrated Brier | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| XGBoost (baseline) | 0.1853 ± 0.0235 | 0.0870 ± 0.0207 | 0.1758 ± 0.0107 | 0.7505 ± 0.0198 |
| Tabular NN | 0.2419 ± 0.0189 | 0.0874 ± 0.0226 | 0.1843 ± 0.0071 | 0.7280 ± 0.0187 |
| Ensemble | 0.1617 ± 0.0181 | 0.0834 ± 0.0154 | 0.1733 ± 0.0091 | 0.7410 ± 0.0164 |

Paired against the baseline on calibrated ECE: the ensemble wins
6/10 seeds (median -0.00394, Wilcoxon p = 0.6953), and the NN alone
wins 5/10 (p = 0.9219). Neither is significant.
On accuracy the ensemble *loses*, 2/8 (median -0.00500, p = 0.1953).

**Verdict: null. Not adopted.**

The informative part is where the gain goes. The ensemble clearly improves
*uncalibrated* ECE — 0.1617 against 0.1853 — and temperature scaling then
absorbs the entire difference. Ensembling and calibration are substitutes
here, not complements. Since the ensemble costs a whole second model family
and calibration costs one parameter, the one-parameter head wins on every
axis that matters.

## Phase B — split conformal prediction (Tier 2)

Temperature scaling yields a point confidence with no guarantee. Split
conformal yields a set with a distribution-free marginal coverage guarantee.
The calibration split is halved so the temperature and the conformal
quantile never see the same points.

| Target | Empirical coverage | Min over seeds | Seeds ≥ target | Avg set size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.80 | 0.7930 ± 0.0399 | 0.7300 | 5/10 | 1.102 ± 0.069 |
| 0.90 | 0.8815 ± 0.0408 | 0.8200 | 4/10 | 1.357 ± 0.100 |
| 0.95 | 0.9335 ± 0.0398 | 0.8550 | 4/10 | 1.570 ± 0.134 |

Coverage tracks the target closely and sits 1–2 percentage points below it at
every level. At n = 10 seeds the shortfall is within sampling noise, so this
is **not** reported as a violated guarantee — but neither is it confirmation.
The most likely cause is that the three-way split is stratified, which mildly
breaks the exchangeability conformal assumes.
`tests/test_conformal.py::test_coverage_guarantee_holds_on_synthetic_exchangeable_data`
checks the theorem on genuinely iid draws, where coverage does meet the target —
so the implementation is right and the data is awkward, rather than the reverse.

Set size is reported alongside coverage throughout, because coverage alone is
trivially satisfiable: always return both labels and you have 100% coverage
and zero information.

### Circuit feasibility gate

| Head | logrows | Rows used | Proving time | Proof size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature only (baseline) | 15 | 39 | 1.72 s | 17,525 B |
| Temperature + conformal | 15 | 95 | 1.66 s | 17,828 B |

Budget is logrows ≤ 15. **PASS** — the conformal head stays at the same logrows, adding 56 rows out of
32,768 and no measurable proving time.

It fits because both comparisons push through the monotone sigmoid into logit
space and become comparisons against two constants:

```
include_1  <=>  1 - p <= q  <=>  z >= logit(1 - q)
include_0  <=>      p <= q  <=>  z <= logit(q)
```

so the sigmoid never enters the circuit and the head stays affine.

**Verdict: promoted to Tier 2.**

**One thing this does not buy.** The quantile `q` is not computed in-circuit —
that needs a sort over calibration scores, and it is a per-deployment constant
rather than a per-decision one. It is fitted off-chain and committed exactly as
the temperature `T` already is, so its provenance is exactly as unproven as
`T`'s. And the coverage guarantee is *marginal*: a conformal predictor can hit
90% overall while covering a protected subgroup at 60%, which is the same
limitation aggregate ECE already has.

## Phase C — deep ensembles for epistemic uncertainty (Tier 1)

5 independently seeded network copies; disagreement is the standard
deviation of member probabilities, fed to the calibration head alongside the
margin.

| Arm | ECE | Brier | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (baseline, 1 param) | 0.0870 ± 0.0207 | 0.1758 ± 0.0107 | 0.7505 ± 0.0198 |
| Margin + disagreement | 0.1072 ± 0.0324 | 0.1916 ± 0.0174 | 0.7295 ± 0.0294 |
| Margin + constant (capacity control) | 0.0743 ± 0.0170 | 0.1757 ± 0.0109 | 0.7530 ± 0.0165 |

The epistemic signal makes calibration **worse**: 2W/8L against the
baseline, median +0.01611, p = 0.1309.

**Verdict: negative. Not adopted.**

The capacity control is what makes this interpretable, and it is the reason it
was included. The *same two-input head shape*, fed a constant zero in place of
the disagreement signal, beats the baseline: 8W/2L, median -0.01238, p = 0.0371.
So the small architectural change helps slightly, and the epistemic signal
then actively destroys that gain. Without the control the result would have
read as "ensemble disagreement doesn't help"; with it, the finding is sharper
and less comfortable — **the signal is noise that the head overfits** on 200
calibration points.

The cost side is not close either: 5 model copies at 53.6 s per seed,
against roughly a tenth of that for the single baseline model, to make
calibration worse.

## Phase D — counterfactual explanations (Tier 1, evidence layer)

Greedy coordinate search over actionable features only, committed by hash
alongside the SHAP vector — the same trust slot, not a new one.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Rejections examined | 40 |
| Counterfactual found | 40 (100%) |
| Mean changes when found | 1.075 |
| Immutable-attribute violations | 0 |
| Implausible (unobserved) values | 0 |
| Mean overlap with SHAP top-5 | 0.762 |
| Hash reproduces on rerun | True |

Immutable attributes (age_years, foreign_worker, n_liable_maintenance, personal_status_sex) are excluded by
construction and the exclusion is asserted, not assumed.

**Verdict: adopted, as evidence only.**

The plausibility check earned its place on the first run, which flagged 10 of
40 cases. Quantile-derived candidate values are interpolated, so the search was
proposing loan amounts that occur nowhere in the data — "reduce the loan to
3,271 DM" is not an actionable instruction when no such loan exists. Candidates
are now snapped to observed values.

The 0.762 overlap with SHAP is a consistency check on the bundle: the two
explanations are committed together, and if they routinely disagreed the
evidence would be internally inconsistent. Note that the search is greedy, so
what is reported is *sparsity*, not minimality.

## Phase E — GNN collusion detection (Tier 1, monitoring)

Bipartite claimant/operator dispute graph, projected to claimant-claimant
co-dispute edges; two-hop GraphSAGE node classifier. Both ring shapes are
generated: *shielding* (confederates farm upheld disputes against their own
operator) and *griefing* (a ring targets a competitor).

| Ring intensity | GNN AUC | Degree-baseline AUC | GNN P@k | Baseline P@k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | 1.000 ± 0.001 | 0.984 ± 0.009 | 0.971 | 0.778 |
| 0.85 | 0.980 ± 0.013 | 0.958 ± 0.014 | 0.765 | 0.608 |
| 0.70 | 0.884 ± 0.097 | 0.865 ± 0.102 | 0.623 | 0.432 |
| 0.60 | 0.856 ± 0.104 | 0.841 ± 0.104 | 0.510 | 0.390 |
| 0.50 | 0.807 ± 0.040 | 0.774 ± 0.066 | 0.412 | 0.310 |

Intensity is the fraction of a ring member's disputes aimed at the ring's
operator. At 1.00 the ring is trivially separable and reporting only that
number would be close to dishonest, so the sweep runs down to 0.50 where the
ring is half camouflaged in ordinary traffic. The degree baseline —
concentration on the top operator — is the control that says whether graph
structure contributes anything beyond a threshold.

**Verdict: wired to an enforcement path, on synthetic validation only.**

**This is the phase with the weakest evidence and the strongest consequences,
and that combination is deliberate on the deployer's part rather than an
oversight.** Labels exist because the rings were injected. There are no
labelled real collusion examples — the protocol has never run — so no
real-world detection performance is claimed or claimable, and the
false-positive rate on genuine dispute traffic is **unknown**.

Despite that, the detector now has an on-chain enforcement path via
`CollusionOracle`. An enforced flag **blocks a claimant from filing disputes**
and **withholds their payout**. The earlier version of this report said no such
wiring existed; it now does, and the honest thing is to say so plainly rather
than to leave the old sentence standing.

### What the enforcement path can and cannot do

| | |
|---|---|
| Can block a claimant from opening disputes | Yes, once the appeal window elapses |
| Can withhold a claimant's awarded payout | Yes, into reversible quarantine |
| Can slash an operator directly | **No.** The slash is unchanged and still needs the committee |
| Can cause permanent loss automatically | **No.** Forfeiture is a separate admin-only act |
| Can be reversed | Yes. Clearing a flag refunds the quarantine in full |

Four containment properties carry the whole design, and each is asserted by a
test rather than described:

1. **A flag never slashes.** The operator's penalty is computed and taken
   identically whether or not the claimant is flagged — a suspected colluder
   does not make a bad decision retroactively good, and must not become a route
   for an operator to escape a slash.
2. **An appeal window precedes any effect.** A zero-length window is rejected at
   construction, so a false positive can never bite in the block it was raised.
3. **Withheld funds are quarantined, not burned and not paid to the operator.**
   Paying the operator would give it a motive to get its own claimants flagged,
   which is a worse incentive than the one this removes.
4. **Confiscation requires a human.** `forfeit` is admin-only and separate from
   flagging, so the only path from a model output to permanent loss runs
   through a person taking a deliberate action.

### The danger, stated as executable tests

`CollusionOracle.t.sol` carries three `test_dangerous_*` cases whose purpose is
to keep this risk from being forgotten:

- `test_dangerous_falsePositiveSilencesAnHonestClaimant` — a wrongly flagged
  claimant cannot challenge a genuinely bad decision, and the operator keeps its
  stake. An unvalidated model output protects a bad decision.
- `test_dangerous_reporterCanFlagArbitrarily` — nothing on-chain distinguishes a
  detector output from a lie. One key, no proof, no N-of-M.
- `test_dangerous_flagPlusForfeitConfiscatesAnHonestAward` — flag, wait, resolve,
  forfeit: a legitimate award is gone, and the only recourse was an appeal to the
  same admin that took it.

**Recommended deployment posture:** pass `address(0)` for the oracle until the
detector's false-positive rate has been measured on the deployment's own
traffic. `test_poolWithoutOracleIgnoresFlagsEntirely` shows that detaching it
costs nothing else. The reporter script defaults to `--dry-run`, refuses to
submit without `--i-understand-this-is-unvalidated`, and caps flags per run —
but those are speed bumps in a script, not properties of the system.

## Phase F — on-chain operator reputation (trust tier: inherited)

An exponential moving average of realised Brier score per operator, updated at
each dispute resolution from the same `(confidence, outcome)` pair that drives
the slash — so the score and the penalty cannot diverge.

**Tier placement is the substance of this phase.** The aggregation is on-chain
and auditable: anyone can replay the update rule against the emitted events.
But the inputs are resolved dispute outcomes, which come from the N-of-M
committee — tier 3. The score therefore inherits the trust level of the dispute
layer beneath it and is worth exactly that much, no more. On-chain does not
mean zk-proved, and a reputation number is not a new cryptographic guarantee.

`test_tierBoundary_colludingCommitteeCanManufactureReputation` asserts this
rather than describing it: two colluding resolvers ruin an honest operator's
calibration record with no recourse.

The obvious gaming path is checked too. An operator farming disputes on
decisions it knows are correct still pays the irreducible `(c−1)²` on every
sample and cannot drive its score to zero — the `p(1−p)` term from the
proposal's §3.2, appearing as contract behaviour.

Wiring is optional: `address(0)` disables recording, and a pool without a
register slashes identically. Reputation can never become load-bearing for the
penalty.

## Phase G — model-version registry (guarantee: content integrity)

A halo2 verifying key covers a *program*, not an *input*: one vkey verifies
every proof from that circuit whatever weights were compiled in. So
`model_version` cannot be inferred from the proof and has to be an explicit
commitment, or an attestation silently means "some version of this head ran".

**What it guarantees:** the artifact referenced by a version id hashes to the
recorded value. Substitution is detectable, from either direction, by anyone.

**What it does not guarantee**, each pinned by a test that would have to be
changed to inflate the claim:

- *Training integrity.* A poisoned model registers and verifies perfectly
  cleanly (`test_boundary_poisonedModelRegistersAndVerifiesCleanly`). Data
  provenance and poisoning are a different and much harder problem, out of
  scope here.
- *That the registered artifact is the one that ran.* The proof does not bind
  the version id to the circuit
  (`test_boundary_anyoneMayReferenceAVersionTheyDidNotRun`) — the same
  input-provenance gap the margin has.

Revocation is advisory and never deletes the record: deleting would
retroactively break verification of historical attestations, which is precisely
the audit trail the registry exists to protect.

## What moved the needle

**Earned their place:** conformal prediction (a genuinely stronger uncertainty
claim, and it fits the circuit budget), counterfactual explanations (directly
serves the adverse-action use case), the reputation register and the model
registry (both close real gaps, both correctly placed outside tier 1).

**Did not:** the heterogeneous ensemble (null, and costs accuracy) and deep-
ensemble epistemic uncertainty (actively harmful, and expensive). Both remain
in the repository as evidence; neither is in the default pipeline.

**Remains unvalidated, and is now enforcing anyway:** the GNN collusion
detector has never seen real collusion, and has been given an enforcement path
regardless. The path is bounded — no direct slashing, an appeal window, and
reversible quarantine — but a false positive still silences a legitimate
claimant for the duration, and the rate at which that happens is unknown.
This is the largest unquantified risk in the system, and it is a deliberate
choice rather than an oversight.

**Unchanged by any of this:** the tier-1 boundary. The input logit is still an
unverified operator-supplied value, and nothing in Phases A–G touches that.
A more sophisticated base model, a stronger uncertainty quantifier, and a
richer evidence bundle all sit on the same unproven input — which is the
point Phase 0 established and this work did not move.
